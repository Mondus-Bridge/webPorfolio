# How to Actually Communicate with Your AI Agent

If you're using AI to solve a problem on a daily basis, you need to learn how to communicate with it effectively. While communication comes naturally to humans, it requires a specific approach when interacting with algorithms. As a practitioner who works directly with AI workflows, I've found that mastering this dialogue is what transforms raw outputs into reliable production work. Keep in mind that just as every person is unique, AI models also vary greatly in how they think and respond.

## 1. The Language of AI

Here's something I've noticed: mainstream AI models like ChatGPT or DeepSeek are naturally biased toward specific languages—mainly English and Mandarin. This is because they are trained on existing web data. It's estimated that English accounts for roughly 50% to 56% of all internet content, while Mandarin holds a massive share as well (around 20%). Because of this, knowing at least one of these languages is essential for getting the best results from AI. While you can converse in your native language, the AI may struggle to understand nuances because its neural representations for that language are much weaker.

## 2. Vibe and Context

Any conversation begins with speaking the same language, but that alone isn't enough. For instance, an ordinary person won't fully understand a lawyer using legal jargon or a chemistry PhD speaking in complex academic terms. It can sound like gibberish and leave the listener completely lost. (If those experts were to simplify their explanations, that would be a topic for another article.) The main point here is that effective communication requires both sides to share the same context and use a compatible style of expression.

## 3. Find Your Model

Every AI model understands inputs differently, but that's only half the story—they also produce very different outputs. You might like one model's style while finding another completely wrong for your project. That's why it pays to experiment with a few. Think of it like talking to people: you won't vibe with everyone, and the same applies here. However, try not to blame the model right away. In my hands-on project exploring [how AI tools can help build a mid-grade web app](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/my-experience-ai-tools-help-create-mid-grade-web-app-ilnur-gabitov-sbpef/), I realized that project success depends heavily on choosing the right model for the job and refining your instructions. Effective communication is a two-way street. Instead of assuming the AI failed, reflect on whether your prompt was clear enough and adjust your approach. You can't just demand, "I want this," and expect a mind reader to deliver perfect results—even a telepath would struggle with vague instructions! Speak clearly and thoughtfully. Explain what you need so simply that even a six-year-old could follow along. That is the real key to a successful session. Don't expect magic—put in the effort, refine your prompts, and keep trying.

> Example in frontend: "In page /main, in header component - add section: 'About US' between other sections: 'Pricing' and 'Tools'. Route of this page should be: /about and on click user should be transferred to that route in the same tab."

> Example in backend: "User has a duplicate data in page: /leaderboards. Here json response {{insert relative path to file or copy paste the data}} and player: 'MiMi' have 2 identical data duplicate. Before exploring the issue, this problem exist in DB table: 'leaderboards_charts' and repeated for some users too. This is not expected behavior and give me a reason why it happened and offer solutions to fix it with full detailed explanations."

## 4. Watch Out for Code-Switching

My second language is Russian. Years ago—back when we only had simple chatbots instead of AI agents—I realized that mixing English and Russian in a single session caused problems. The output quality dropped significantly, and the model started hallucinating. Unfortunately, I accidentally made that same mistake in a recent session. To fix it, I instructed the AI agent to treat my Russian input strictly as raw data to be converted into an article, and explicitly told it not to switch the main conversation language from English to Russian.

## Summary

Final thought: You can certainly still interact with AI in your native language; this article simply reflects my personal experience. For casual or simple everyday tasks, high-level prompt strategy isn't strictly necessary. But if you are a professional or AI enthusiast, learning these subtle mechanics will help you truly master the tool.
